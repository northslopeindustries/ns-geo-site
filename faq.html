Frequently Asked Questions

Does it matter which side of the fabric goes against the ground?

Yes. For most installations, place the soft side down. This helps pull water through the fabric more effectively. On slopes or hills, however, you may want to flip it so the soft side faces up instead; the texture helps grip the mulch on top and keeps it from sliding or washing away.

How far apart should I put my staples or pins?

As a general rule, place staples or pins every 3 to 4 feet along all edges and seams, with tighter spacing in windy areas or on slopes to prevent shifting.

How do I know which fabric weight/type to use for my project?

It depends on what you're trying to solve. Our Pro Guard 20 woven fabric comes in a single weight, 5 oz, and is the right choice when weed control is your main concern: garden beds, pathways, and under gravel or mulch.

Use our non-woven filter fabric for soil stabilization, erosion control, or drainage. Common uses would be for retaining walls or French drains. Our Filter Fabric product page has more details on weights and use cases for that product.

Can I put mulch or rock directly on top of the fabric?

Yes. That's the standard use case. Landscape fabric is designed to sit under a layer of mulch, rock, or gravel. The fabric blocks weeds from below while the material on top holds it in place, blocks sunlight, and protects the fabric from UV breakdown and foot traffic.

How long will Pro Guard 20 last before it needs replacing?

Pro Guard 20 is rated for a 20-year lifespan, as long as it's covered properly with mulch, rock, or gravel. Leaving it exposed to direct sunlight without cover will shorten its life.

Is landscape fabric really better than plastic sheeting?

Generally, yes, for most landscaping uses. Plastic sheeting is solid, so it blocks water and air along with weeds. That starves the soil underneath, which can harm nearby plants over time and cause water to pool or run off instead of soaking in. Landscape fabric is porous: it blocks weeds and light while still letting water, air, and nutrients reach the soil.

What's the difference between woven and non-woven fabric?

Woven fabric is made from strands crossed over each other, like a tight mesh. It's built for weed control and holds up well under foot traffic, gravel, or mulch. This is what our Pro Guard 20 is made from, and it's the right choice for garden beds, pathways, and general weed barrier use.

Non-woven fabric is made from fibers pressed together into a sheet, similar to felt. It's built more for soil stabilization, erosion control, and drainage rather than weed control, common in applications like retaining walls or French drains. This is what our filter fabric is made from.

Will weeds still grow on top of the fabric?

It's possible, though the fabric itself isn't the cause. Over time, dust, mulch breakdown, and windblown seeds can build up on top of the fabric, creating a thin layer of soil where weeds can take root, even though the fabric below is still blocking weeds from growing up through it. Because the fabric prevents those weeds from rooting into the ground underneath, any weeds that do appear on top should be extremely easy to pull out. Keeping mulch topped up and removing any surface debris helps minimize this even further.
